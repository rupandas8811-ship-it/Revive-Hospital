Clinical Assessment

Signs and Symptoms of Appendicitis

Appendicitis develops rapidly. Recognizing these symptoms early allows for timely intervention before complications occur.

01

Right Lower Abdominal Pain

Pain often begins as a dull ache around the belly button and shifts sharply to the lower right abdomen (McBurney's point).

02

Pain Aggravated by Movement

Sharp discomfort that intensifies when walking, coughing, taking deep breaths, or making sudden movements.

03

Nausea, Vomiting & Appetite Loss

Inability to eat, persistent nausea, and vomiting shortly after the onset of abdominal distress.

04

Low-Grade Fever & Chills

A rising fever, shivering, and rapid heartbeat as the body reacts to inflammation in the appendix tissue.

05

Abdominal Tenderness & Bloating

Marked tenderness on gentle pressing of the right abdomen, sometimes with abdominal rigidity and swelling.

06

Digestive Changes

Constipation, difficulty passing gas, or occasional diarrhea accompanying the sudden stomach distress.

Medical FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions About Appendix Care

Medically reviewed answers to help you understand appendicitis symptoms and treatment options.

Call 089510 16007
How do I know if my abdominal pain is appendicitis?

Appendicitis pain typically starts around the navel and shifts within hours to the lower right side of the abdomen. The pain worsens when coughing, walking, or pressing on the area. If accompanied by nausea or fever, prompt medical assessment is essential.

Can appendicitis be treated with antibiotics alone?

While mild cases are occasionally studied with antibiotic therapy, surgery (appendectomy) remains the definitive, safest standard of care worldwide because of high recurrence rates and the risk of sudden perforation.

What are the advantages of laparoscopic appendectomy?

Laparoscopic keyhole surgery uses three tiny punctures rather than a large open cut. It results in substantially less pain, minimal scarring, a lower risk of infection, and a faster return to everyday life.

What happens if an inflamed appendix is left untreated?

If left untreated, an inflamed appendix can burst (perforate), spilling bacteria and infected contents into the abdominal cavity (peritonitis) or causing an abscess. This is a life-threatening complication requiring emergency surgery.

How long is the hospital stay after appendix surgery?

With uncomplicated laparoscopic surgery, most patients are discharged within 24 to 48 hours and can comfortably resume light activities and diet within a few days.
