Common Symptoms of Piles

Piles (haemorrhoids) are swollen blood vessels in the lower rectum or anal opening. Symptoms vary depending on whether they are internal or external.

01

Painless Bleeding

Bright red blood on toilet paper or in the toilet bowl during or after a bowel movement is one of the most common early signs.

02

Swelling or Lumps

A tender, swollen lump or protruding tissue near the anus that may retract spontaneously or require manual reduction.

03

Itching & Discomfort

Persistent irritation, itching (pruritus ani), mucus leakage, and a feeling of incomplete bowel evacuation.

i Rectal bleeding should always be evaluated by a medical clinician to rule out other gastrointestinal conditions.

Understanding Piles Treatment Options

Not every patient with piles requires surgery. Treatment recommendations at Revive Healthcare Hospital depend entirely on the clinical grade and symptom severity.

  • ✓Medical & Dietary GuidanceHigh-fiber recommendations, adequate hydration, stool softeners, and soothing topical treatments for early stages.
  • ✓Office-based ProceduresRubber band ligation or sclerotherapy when conservative measures are insufficient.
  • ✓Advanced Surgical OptionsMinimally invasive stapler hemorrhoidopexy (MIPH), laser procedures, or surgical excision for severe prolapsing piles.
  • ✓Supportive Post-Procedure CareComprehensive dietary advice and follow-up guidance to prevent symptom recurrence.

Frequently Asked Questions About Piles

Helpful clinical answers to common questions about haemorrhoid symptoms, evaluation, and treatments.

What symptoms should prompt a consultation for piles?

Painless bright red bleeding during bowel movements, recurring lumps or swelling around the anus, persistent itching, or anal discomfort warrant a professional clinical assessment.

Do all patients with piles need surgery?

No. Early-stage piles (Grades 1 and 2) are typically managed with high-fiber diets, adequate hydration, stool softeners, and medication. Surgery is advised only when conservative methods are insufficient or for advanced prolapsing piles.

What is the difference between internal and external piles?

Internal piles arise inside the lower rectum and are usually painless, often presenting with bleeding. External piles develop under the skin around the anus and may become tender, swollen, or painful if a blood clot forms (thrombosed pile).

What happens during the initial piles evaluation?

The clinician will take a detailed medical history and carry out a gentle physical examination (such as visual inspection and proctoscopy) to determine the exact grade and plan appropriate treatment.

Can piles recur after treatment?

Following proper lifestyle modifications—such as maintaining regular bowel habits, eating high-fiber foods, and avoiding prolonged straining—significantly reduces the risk of recurrence.
