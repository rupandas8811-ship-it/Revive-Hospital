Signs of Anal Fissure

Symptoms & When to Seek Medical Evaluation

An anal fissure causes noticeable distress during bowel movements. Proper differentiation from piles is essential for accurate treatment.

01

Sharp, Tearing Pain

Intense, sharp burning or cutting pain during bowel movements that often lingers for hours afterward due to sphincter spasms.

02

Bright Red Bleeding

Streaks of bright red blood visible on the surface of hard stools or on toilet paper after wiping.

03

Visible Crack or Skin Tag

A visible small tear in the anal margin, often accompanied in chronic cases by a small skin tag known as a sentinel pile.

i If bowel movements cause persistent dread or severe lingering pain, do not delay clinical evaluation.

Medical Management

How Anal Fissures are Evaluated and Managed

The key to healing an anal fissure is relaxing the hypertonic internal anal sphincter muscle to restore blood flow and allow the tear to heal.

  • ✓Prescription OintmentsMedications (such as diltiazem or nitroglycerin based creams) designed to relax sphincter muscle spasms and improve local blood circulation.
  • ✓Sitz BathsWarm water soaks to ease muscular tension and soothe the area after bowel movements.
  • ✓Stool Softening & Dietary FiberEnsuring bulky, soft stools to prevent stretching and re-tearing of healing anal tissue.
  • ✓Care for Chronic FissuresFor fissures that do not heal after weeks of conservative therapy, minor surgical procedures (such as lateral internal sphincterotomy) or Botox can be evaluated.

What causes an anal fissure to develop?

Fissures most commonly occur when passing large, hard, or dry stools that overstretch the anal mucosa. Chronic diarrhea, straining, or tight anal sphincter muscles can also contribute.

How is a fissure different from piles (haemorrhoids)?

While both can cause bleeding, an anal fissure is characterized by sharp, severe, tearing pain during and after bowel movements, whereas early piles are typically painless unless complicated by a blood clot.

Can an anal fissure heal without surgery?

Yes, the majority of acute fissures heal within a few weeks with appropriate conservative management: topical relaxant ointments, sitz baths, adequate dietary fiber, and plenty of fluids.

What should I avoid if I suspect an anal fissure?

Avoid straining during bowel movements, ignoring the urge to pass stool, eating spicy foods that irritate the bowel, and using harsh dry toilet paper.

When is an anal fissure considered chronic?

An anal fissure that persists beyond six to eight weeks despite conservative care is considered chronic and may require specialist intervention to break the pain-spasm cycle.
