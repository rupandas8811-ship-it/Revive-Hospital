Anal Fissure Symptoms

How to Recognize an Anal Fissure — Key Warning Signs

An anal fissure is a tear in the sensitive mucosa of the lower anal canal, commonly triggered by passing hard stool. Identifying the classic symptoms differentiates fissures from piles and prevents progression to a chronic condition.

01

Sharp "Glass-Cut" Pain During & After Stool

Intense, sharp burning or cutting sensation during bowel movements that often lingers as a dull, throbbing ache for minutes or hours due to internal sphincter muscle spasms.

02

Bright Red Streaks of Blood

Fresh, bright red blood visible on the surface of hard stools, on the toilet tissue when wiping, or dripping into the toilet bowl, distinct from deeper gastrointestinal bleeding.

03

Chronic Spasms & Sentinel Skin Tag

In chronic fissures lasting beyond 6 weeks, constant sphincter spasms prevent capillary blood flow, forming a small fibrous skin tag (sentinel pile) or swollen papilla at the tear edge.

i Clinical Guidance: Avoiding bowel movements out of fear of pain only worsens constipation and deepens tears. Schedule a gentle, confidential evaluation to break the cycle.

How is an anal fissure different from piles (hemorrhoids)?

While both conditions can produce bright red blood, an anal fissure causes intense, sharp, burning pain during and for several hours after defecation due to internal sphincter muscle spasms. In contrast, uncomplicated internal piles are generally painless swelling of venous cushions unless thrombosed or prolapsed.

Can an anal fissure heal on its own without surgery?

Yes. Many acute anal fissures heal within 4 to 6 weeks when treated early with conservative medical management: prescription muscle-relaxing ointments (such as diltiazem), warm water sitz baths, stool softeners, adequate hydration, and a high-fiber diet.

When is laser treatment or surgery recommended for an anal fissure?

When a fissure persists beyond 6 to 8 weeks despite medical management, it is classified as chronic. At this stage, fibrosis and persistent sphincter spasms prevent blood flow. A precise laser sphincter release or fissurectomy breaks the spasm cycle, restores blood supply, and enables the tissue to heal permanently.

Find out whether day-care surgery is suitable for your fissure condition?

Most patients needing minor intervention or laser sphincterotomy are eligible for day-care surgery. Following clinical assessment, patients are treated under gentle local or short anesthesia and safely discharged the same day, returning to comfortable routines within 24 to 48 hours.

Will laser sphincter treatment affect my bowel control (continence)?

No. Our specialized proctology surgeons perform precise, controlled micro-release of only the hypertonic internal sphincter fibers without disturbing the voluntary external anal sphincter, safeguarding normal bowel control and continence.
