Biliary Colic Symptoms

How to Recognize Gallstone Pain (Biliary Colic)

Gallstones often remain dormant until a stone temporarily blocks the gallbladder duct, triggering sudden, intense abdominal distress that requires medical assessment.

01

Sudden Upper Right Abdominal Pain

Rapidly intensifying, sharp or cramping pain in the upper right quadrant or center of the abdomen, frequently occurring after eating oily or heavy meals.

02

Radiating Back & Shoulder Ache

Pain that spreads straight through to the upper back, between the shoulder blades, or under the right shoulder, lingering from thirty minutes to several hours.

03

Nausea, Bloating & Indigestion

Recurring digestive intolerance, persistent bloated feeling after eating small portions, nausea, and vomiting during acute gallbladder spasm episodes.

i Emergency Warning: Persistent pain lasting more than 4–5 hours, high fever with chills, or yellowing of the eyes/skin (jaundice) indicates acute inflammation (cholecystitis) or bile duct blockage and requires immediate hospital emergency care.

Frequently Asked Questions About Gallstones

Common questions answered by medical clinicians regarding diagnosis, gallbladder removal, and diet.

Do all gallstones require gallbladder removal surgery?

No. Gallstones that do not cause any symptoms (silent gallstones) typically do not require surgery and are managed with observation and lifestyle monitoring. Surgery is recommended when gallstones cause pain, inflammation, or complications.

Can gallstones simply be dissolved or crushed with medicine?

Medications to dissolve cholesterol gallstones have very limited success and require years of use, with high recurrence rates once discontinued. For symptomatic gallstones, laparoscopic cholecystectomy remains the gold standard treatment worldwide.

Can gallbladder removal be done as a day-care procedure?

Yes, many patients undergoing elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy are suitable for day-care or short-stay surgery, allowing discharge the same day or within 24 hours depending on the surgeon's clinical evaluation and general fitness.

Can a person live a normal life without a gallbladder?

Yes. The liver produces bile continuously. Without a gallbladder, bile flows directly into the small intestine to digest food. Most patients adjust rapidly and experience no long-term dietary restrictions.

What are the emergency signs of gallstone complications?

Severe persistent abdominal pain lasting more than 5 hours, fever with chills, yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice), or severe vomiting indicate potential infection or duct blockage and require emergency evaluation.
