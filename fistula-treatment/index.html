Fistula Warning Signs

How to Recognize an Anal Fistula — Key Symptoms

An anal fistula usually develops following an unresolved perianal abscess. Recognizing recurring drainage or discomfort helps avoid recurrent infections and tract branching.

01

Continuous or Intermittent Discharge

Persistent leaking of pus, watery fluid, or blood from a tiny external opening near the anus, frequently soiling undergarments and causing an unpleasant odor.

02

Recurring Abscesses & Boils

Painful, throbbing swellings near the anus that periodically swell up, burst, drain fluid, and then refill over weeks or months if left unsealed.

03

Perianal Skin Irritation & Pain

Persistent soreness, itching (pruritus ani), and excoriated skin from constant fluid discharge, accompanied by dull aching while sitting or during bowel movements.

i Important Note: High fever, chills, or rapidly spreading redness and swelling around the perineum indicate an acute deep abscess requiring urgent hospital drainage.

Surgical Solutions

Advanced Fistula Repair Options at Revive

Because the internal opening of a fistula is continuously exposed to bowel bacteria, definitive surgical closure is necessary. Our surgical team prioritizes sphincter-sparing procedures to protect natural continence.

  • ✓FiLaC® Radial Laser ClosureA flexible radial laser fiber delivers controlled thermal energy along the tract, collapsing and sealing the tunnel from inside out without cutting sphincter muscles.
  • ✓LIFT (Ligation of Intersphincteric Fistula Tract)A sophisticated muscle-sparing technique that isolates and ties off the tract between the sphincter muscles with zero risk to bowel control.
  • ✓Ask About Day-Care Surgery SuitabilityEligible patients undergoing FiLaC laser or select sphincter-preserving repairs can often be discharged the same day or within 24 hours based on post-op evaluation.
  • ✓Structured Post-Procedure Wound CareGuidance on sitz baths, dressing hygiene, and dietary fiber to promote painless healing and prevent tract recurrence.

Frequently Asked Questions About Anal Fistula

Can an anal fistula heal on its own or with antibiotics?

Antibiotics can control temporary acute flare-ups or infections, but they do not eliminate the underlying epithelialized tract. Surgical intervention by a qualified clinician is usually required for definitive resolution.

How is bowel control (continence) protected during fistula surgery?

Our proctologists specialize in sphincter-sparing procedures such as FiLaC (Fistula-tract Laser Closure) and LIFT (Ligation of Intersphincteric Fistula Tract). These advanced techniques close the tunnel while leaving the anal sphincter muscle complex intact.

Can fistula treatment be done as a daycare procedure?

Yes, many laser fistula (FiLaC) and select sphincter-sparing procedures are performed under daycare protocols, allowing eligible patients to go home the same day or within 24 hours based on clinical evaluation.

What is the advantage of FiLaC laser treatment for fistula?

FiLaC uses a 360-degree radial laser fiber inserted directly into the tract. It delivers thermal energy that collapses the tunnel without any cutting of the anal sphincter, promoting faster recovery with minimal postoperative discomfort.

What happens if a fistula is left untreated?

Leaving a fistula untreated can lead to recurrent painful abscesses, branching of the tract into complex extensions (horseshoe fistula), and increased risk of surrounding tissue infection.
